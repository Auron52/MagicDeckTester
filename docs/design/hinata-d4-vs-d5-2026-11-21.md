# Hinata: why depth 4 measured better than depth 5 (2026-11-21/22)

Status: DIAGNOSED. The settings decision (depth, ladder shape, escalation beam) is the USER's; nothing here is adopted.
Branch `wip/hinata-d4-d5`; binary = Release of src tree `94df5afc7` (origin/phase-1-2-deck-analyzer `906d3c0a4`), no
source changes. Scratch (regenerable): `build/scratch/claude_hinata_d4d5/`.

## The question (USER, verbatim, 2026-11-21)

"This doesn't make much sense. We shouldn't be getting better results with Depth 4 than Depth 5 unless perhaps this
is just variance. If they are similarily constrained below 4 turns lookahead they should act the same and going
further should not make the results worse. We need to dig into this rather than just wave it off."

## Answer in one paragraph

It is not variance, and it is not depth. Two things differ between the "d4" and "d5" Hinata configurations besides
the target depth, and each one costs quality at the shipped b30 budget:

1. **Ladder shape.** The 2026-11-11 d4 block (E.7) ran the *emulated* ladder (every decision commits a heuristic-
   rollout line, `ladder: "emulated"` + `emul_r`). The shipped d5 block runs the *hybrid* (no ladder key): the
   value-leaf probe takes most of the budget and the heuristic escalation runs from the remainder. This is the larger
   part of the gap and it is gone by b120.
2. **The escalation beam is switched on by `depth == 5` exactly** (`src/ai/TurnSolver.cpp`, "DEPTH-ADAPTIVE BEAM",
   ~line 125071). At depth 5 the deck's value-ranked beam (Hinata: `beam_width 3, beam_leafdepth 2`) plus probe value
   recording are armed; at depth 4 the beam is OFF. So "d5" also meant "W3 value-ranked beam" -- a lossy prune applied
   at every budget -- and that is the whole depth residual. **With the beam off, d5 plays IDENTICALLY to d4**: on 30
   held-out games (s61001 gi 0..29, emulated ladder) all 30 game digests are equal, and units are 58.5K vs 58.3K per
   game (beam on: 163.7K). This is exactly the USER's expectation -- similarly constrained, they act the same.

## 3. Mechanism (Q4)

### 3a. Shape: the hybrid starves its own escalation at b30
MTG_HYBRID_STATS, 150 games s61001, d5N: 436 decisions, 273 redos; escalations taken 256 / discarded 17; the probe's
leftover at escalation is under 10% of the decision budget in 152 of 272 escalations. The emulated ladder always
commits a heuristic-rollout line. Traced example (smoke s1001 gi22 T1): the hybrid probe ran value passes H1..H4
(72K units, 2.7x the 27K limit) and committed a value-leaf line; the emulated ladder committed a heuristic X1 line
(7K units). d5E reproduces d4E on all 10 GT-slower smoke games; d5N is slower on 9 of 10.

**s5005 gi384** (the overnight's +3 case) is shape too: at b30, d4E wins T4 and d5N T7. T1: d4E's X1 heuristic pass
(30K units) finds Gamble -> Irencrag Feat and a T4 kill; d5N's probe runs H1/H2 value passes only (6.8K units) and
commits Sol Ring. At d8 b0 old sidecar T4 / current sidecar T5, so the value-leaf line choice persists without a
budget. **s6006 gi156** is T5 under both sidecars at b30 and at d8 b0: an engine/binary change, not the setting.

### 3b. Depth: the depth-5-only W3 value beam makes the same heuristic pass cost more
Eight held-out games where d5E is slower than d4E (s61001 gi23/159/176, s62002 gi164, s63003 gi48/392, s64004
gi479/529), all reproduced single-threaded: d5E uses 3-16x the units (e.g. gi23 99.6K -> 1.63M, gi176 402K -> 1.53M).
At the first diverging decision of every one, the same heuristic pass at the same pass depth on the same state costs
more under d5E: s61177 T4 X1 51K -> 936K units; s61024 T4 X3 8.3K -> 86.7K; s62166 T4 X1 9.7K -> 45K; s63051 T2 X2
12K (d4E reaches X2) vs X1 at 53.6K (d5E stops at X1). The start gate then refuses the next pass and d5E commits a
SHALLOWER line (150 games: d5E committed d1 290 / d2 251 / d3 61 / d4 23 / d5 18 of 643, vs d4E d1 189 / d2 185 / d3
101 / d4 82 of 557), and cross-decision reuse drops (game-line-cache nowin_hits 9508 -> 3215). Two
code paths the beam arms could explain the extra cost (not separately measured): the value-ranked reorder replaces
the provider's move order inside the escalation (the first win is found later, so the exact cuts bind later), and
value recording turns off the strict FS cut and the DVS tail-skip during the probe (`FsChildCut`,
`_dvs_dirty.no_keep`).

**The confirming probe** (MTG_ESC_BEAM=0, 30 games s61001, 1 thread):

|---|---|---|---|
| d4E | 5.4000 | 58,323 | -- |

So the depth parameter itself changes nothing at b30 on these games; the beam that depth 5 switches on does.

## 5. What the USER decides

Nothing is adopted. The shipped Hinata setting stays d5N until the USER decides.

1. **The structural question is answered.** The ONLY thing in the engine that makes depth 5 differ from depth 4 is the
   escalation beam's `depth == 5` gate (`TurnSolver.cpp` DEPTH-ADAPTIVE BEAM). With it off, d5 == d4 (emulated: 2398 of
   2399 identical, 1 faster). The beam was adopted 2026-07-18 (W2 -> W3, `escalation-beam-verify.md`) on an engine
   whose Hinata average was ~6.2; on today's engine it costs quality at d5 in both shapes. It is also a lossy prune
   that applies at every budget, which the 2026-10-06 completeness rule ("everything should be findable at unlimited
   or large budgets") does not allow in that form.
2. **Candidate A: `d5E0`** = target_depth 5, `ladder: "emulated"` + `emul_r [1096.28, 256.6, 100.58, 105.9]`,
   `beam_width: 0`. b30 held-out **-0.0734 vs shipped (t -8.7), at 0.74x shipped CPU** (4,070 vs 5,506 ms/game).
   Slower-game bar: 67/70 recover, 1 is a d8-b0 miss that every sidecar shares, 1 abandoned at 50M units, 1 pending.
   This is the recommended one: better and cheaper.
3. **Candidate B: `d5N0`** = the shipped hybrid with `beam_width: 0`. -0.0267 (t -3.1) at **1.17x** CPU. Under the
   cut-CPU rule this does not qualify on its own; listed for completeness. Slower-game bar 77/81, 4 abandoned.
4. **Also the USER's:** whether the beam's depth gate should be removed from the code (so no depth can again silently
   switch on a different search), versus just setting `beam_width: 0` in Hinata's `value_play`. Three other decks ship a d5
   W3 ld2 value beam (Anti-Lifegain, Creature Giving, Dragonstorm); they have the same depth-5-only behaviour and were
   not measured here.
5. Not done here: 2HG for the candidates (the earlier 2HG b30 sample had d5E - d4E 0.000 and d5N - d5E +0.055), the
   b120/b480 rows for d5E0/d5N0, and a smoke/regression GT accept (only needed if one is adopted).

## 6. gi33 (s61001 gi33 = game seed 61034): unpriced enumeration -- a performance defect, not d4/d5

Every arm and budget plays this game at ~20K-90K units but 15 min to 8+ h of CPU, and loses (wt 8). Uncontended, 1
thread, d5N b30: **40,009 units, 926-1130 s CPU** = 0.04 units/ms, against ~12 units/ms for ordinary Hinata games
(d5E beam-off probe: 1.756M units in 150 s CPU) -- the unit price is ~270x too low here, so no budget can bind. RSS
reaches 1.7 GB+ at b30 (5 GB+ at higher budgets; it killed a 4 GB-capped run) and the plan cache fills (3131M/3200M).

The opening hand is Soulfire Eruption, 2x Reality Spasm, Mountain, 2x Forbidden Orchard, Reflecting Pool; the deck
plays a land a turn until T6 (Ponder), T7 Ponder, T8 Soulfire. Each decision stays well under its 27K-unit limit (T2
16.1K, T3 4.8K, T4 11.3K) but takes minutes.

perf (dwarf call graph, whole game): EnumeratePlansCore 38% inclusive, ApplyPlanDirect 37%, BpDeriveContinuationList
31%, AppendSubdecisionAxes 24% (5.8% self), Action/Plan copy+destroy ~12%, TapForCost* 5.5%. The enumerated lists are
enormous: `[pb-lazy-cands] lazy_lists=1911 clones_not_materialised=138,467,970` = **~72,500 deferred clones per
list**, against ~26 per list over 30 ordinary games (42,016 lists, 1.1M clones), and bp-derive refused 1.59M subset
visits (43K over the 30 ordinary games). The lazy layout keeps the memory of those clones down but the axes still
build them and the derivations still walk them, and none of that is charged to the decision budget: a unit is charged
per searched node / rollout, not per enumerated candidate.

It is a defect of the same shape as the reflection-fan runaway fixed on 2026-11-19 (`RfxFanChargeProbe`,
MTG_RFX_FAN_CHARGE: an uncharged fan that the budget could not see). Two parts: (a) PRICING -- enumeration work must be
billed in proportion to the candidates built/walked (a pure re-pricing, inert at an unlimited budget), and the fan
should poll `gamework::Poll()` / `Overrun()` like the reflection fans; (b) BRANCHING -- which axis fans to ~72K per
list on this hand is not yet attributed (the Ponder order fan joins the same lazy layout, kFanPonder; the put-back
axis, the Soulfire own-target axis and the legend-keep fan run in AppendSubdecisionAxes). Next step: a 1-thread gi33
run with a per-axis clone counter (or MTG_PB_LAZY_PONDER=0 / MTG_CAND_CENSUS) to name the axis, then decide whether
the fan holds duplicates (fold it, sound) or is genuinely wide (price it). Not done in this investigation.
