## 2. The slowest turns, and what they are actually doing

Targets are the three most expensive games of that A/B, replayed **single-threaded** (the per-decision
census diffs process-global counters, so a second game in flight makes every delta garbage; rows
carry a `contended` column and the analyzer refuses to rank them).

`python3 scripts/turn_census.py heavyturn` on seed 934087 gi87 (789k units, 26 s single-threaded):

```
    seed   t roots       units   xbud trunc   discarded        ms   pay solves     cands        walkB
  934087   3     1     225,185    25x     1     225,185    14,427   15,743,907    50,832   20,002,295
  934087   6     1     171,848    19x     0           0     9,740    9,526,496    78,728   10,900,522
  934087   4     2     147,566    16x     0           0     8,102    8,289,228    38,560   10,439,870
  934087   5     2     113,549    13x     0           0     3,930    3,708,213    30,799    4,132,442
  934087   2     1     104,873    12x     0           0     9,639   10,683,346    21,740   13,810,425
```

Three facts, each of which is its own finding:

**(a) The budget is nominal.** The cell's budget is 10 virtual ms = **9,000 units**. Over 33 census
decisions: 63.6% of decisions exceed it, 36.4% exceed it **10x**, and 9.1% exceed it 20x. A budget
bounds one PASS, not a decision, and `MTG_DECISION_WORK_X` (the per-decision total ceiling) is
default-disarmed in play. The only thing that stops a runaway decision is the proportional overrun
guard at `kOverrunBudgetMult = 25`.

**(b) 10.1% of all units are spent and then thrown away.** Turn 3 above hit that 25x guard: its
**entire 225,185 units — 14.4 s of a 26 s game — were rolled back**, and `idwaste_rescuable=1` says
the aborted pass had already proven a better win than the one that got committed. (The win itself is
salvaged: `MTG_ID_ANYTIME` has been default ON since 2026-09-09. The *work* is not.)

**(c) One Snow unit costs 60–130x what the knob assumes.** `NODES_PER_VIRTUAL_MS = 900` says a unit
is ~1.1 µs. These games run at **7–15 units/ms**, i.e. 70–140 µs per unit, because a charged unit
drags 24 payment solves behind it at the median and **37 on the slowest decile**. That is why a "10
virtual ms" decision really takes ~1 s of wall, and why a 900 M-unit value-leaf label ceiling means
9–23 **hours** on this deck (`snow-breakpoint-degeneracy.md` §"What was happening").

Efficiency also decays on the tail, which is the question the census was built for: continuation-memo
hit rate 0.860 → 0.809, payment solves per unit 23.999 → 37.321, median → slowest decile.

## 3. The wasted work, found — and it is real but cheap

`turn_census.py explain` on that 14.4 s decision prints its whole counter set. The dominant line:

| counter | value | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `sub_entered` | 20,002,295 | subsets the greedy walk constructed |
| `sub_rej_dupsrc` / `dupc_fold_prefix` | **13,004,839 (65.0%)** | rejected by ONE rule |
| `walk_scored` | 3,289,528 | only **16.4%** of visits are ever scored |
| `pay_calls` | 15,743,907 | ~310 payment solves per scored candidate |
| `drop_unpaid` + `drop_tapped` | 44,845 | activations enumerated, applied, unpayable, silently untapped |
| `snow_look_colfail` | 32,411 / 123,968 | 26% of site-8 opens cannot pay the found card's colours |

The 65% is `SubsetHasDuplicateSacSource`'s **canonical-prefix clause**: among activations sharing an
`equiv_tag` the sources are proved interchangeable, so only a prefix selection {0..k−1} is canonical.
Snow runs 4 Scrying Sheets + 4 Frost Augur and **each activation is its own odometer group**
(`ActivationFamilyKey` → `-1000 - sac_source_id`), so two classes of four give 2⁴ × 2⁴ = 256 digit
combinations of which only 5 × 5 = 25 can survive. The rule was enforced at the subset LEAF — so a
non-canonical combination of digits still ran its entire inner `2^num_ind` loop, built `sel` and
called `consider()` for every position, each rejected for a reason that was decided before the inner
loop began.

**BUILT: `MTG_FOLD_ODO_SKIP` (default ON, heurarm slot `FOLD_ODO_SKIP`)** hoists that clause to the
odometer digit, the same move `MTG_EQUIP_PIECE_DEPS` already makes for stranded equips.
`BuildFoldPrefixMap` is a choice-independent precompute (per enumeration); `FoldPrefixViolated` is
one AND + popcount + compare per class per position. It disarms entirely if a tagged action sits in
`independent` or `auto_sel`, where it could supply a missing predecessor ord.

**Verified byte-identical**: d0 500 games and d3 60 games both report identical digests AND identical
units (2,535,822 both arms). **And it is worth −1.3%** (25.32 s → 24.94 s on the heavy game, 2 reps
interleaved, single-threaded).

> **State the ceiling honestly: 13 M removed visits buy 1.3%.** Each rejected visit costs ~25 ns —
> the clause sits early in `consider()` and `sel` is short — so 65% of the walk's *visits* is under
> 2% of its *time*. This is the `MTG_CAND_DEDUP` lesson again (fires, byte-identical, not faster):
> **a visit count is not a cost.** Keep the skip because it is free and provably inert, not because
> it is the answer.

### The bug this nearly shipped, and the rule it earns

The first version put the skip at BOTH odometer sites. It measured as a 7% unit reduction with a
MOVED d3 digest, while d0 stayed byte-identical. Cause: the leaf clause is guarded by
`from_odometer`, and the `EnumeratePlans` walk sets that flag only under **`MTG_FOLD_SEARCH_ODO`,
which is default OFF** — so by default the search KEEPS non-canonical arrangements there as real
plans, and skipping them at the digit silently deleted plans the engine would have scored
(−1,832 … −58,080 `enum_emitted` per decision). The greedy walk always sets the flag, which is
exactly why d0 could not see it.

**A HOISTED PREDICATE MUST MIRROR THE LEAF'S GATE, NOT ONLY ITS TEST.** The twin is now gated on
`FoldSearchOdometerOn()` and is inert until that reserved lever is armed. `MTG_FOLD_ODO_VERIFY=1`
is the audit that settles it: it stops skipping, runs the leaf clause on the COMPLETE selection, and
prints any position the digit test would have removed but the leaf would have kept. **0 mismatches**
at the greedy site.

## 4. Where the time IS: ~45% of Snow's CPU is mana payment

`perf record -F 499` over a 16 s window of the heavy game on `build/Profile` (flat, self-time; the
DWARF call-graph variant cannot write in this container, and `perf.data` must go to local disk —

| family | self % |
|---|---|
| **payment / tapping** | **39.0%** (+ ~6% in the tail: `PaySacSpendableNow`, `SnapPayFields`, `CommitPaySacSacrifices`, `ActLineHoldMask`, `SourceMaxNetLive`, `PermanentManaYield`, `AnyColorFilterFedSlots`) |

| state copies + sim keys | 7.4% (`__memmove_avx512` 3.0%, `BuildSimKey` 1.7%) |
| simulation proper | 4.1% |
| card DB lookups | 3.5% |

Top symbols: `TapForCostSharedOnceImpl`'s colour lambda **7.29%**, `SolveUncached` 5.81%,

decision, and it is reached once or twice per **scored subset** — of which the greedy walk scores
2.27 M per decision against the search's 36 K candidates.

## 5. Honest charging: the 2.0–2.5x lever, and what it costs

> **⚠ EVERY core-ms RATIO IN THIS SECTION IS BIASED — see §8.2.** Its runs emitted all of one
> arm before any of the next, and the batch scheduler dispatches ties in manifest order, so the
> uncharged baseline ran into the pool's crunch and each charged arm as the pool drained.
> Measured, that ordering reads a median 0.799x on arms doing provably identical work. The
> charged arm was emitted LAST here, so the savings below are overstated. §8.4 re-measures them
> arms-paired with a null arm: the real fleet-wide figure at this charge (W=1) is 0.614x, not
> 0.41–0.51x, and the quality column is the part that held up. Snow's own re-measurement is
> 0.451x at d3/b10. The §5 *conclusions* survive; its numbers do not.


`MTG_SOLVE_CHARGE` bills the greedy subset walk against the budget that is supposed to bound it
(one unit per `consider` visit). It has existed, default-off and unused, since the Melira keepgen
work; it is now a heurarm slot (`SOLVE_CHARGE`) so both arms share ONE pooled batch.

Same 1,920 paired games, `logs/snowperf/chargeab.sh`:

| cell | core-ms OFF | core-ms ON | ratio | Δ turns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| d2/b1 | 937,320 | 476,722 | **0.509x** | +0.0125 |
| d3/b10 | 1,490,750 | 680,107 | **0.456x** | +0.0163 |
| d5/b20 | 792,982 | 323,114 | **0.407x** | +0.0094 |

**2.0–2.5x cheaper, and the quality cost is real, not noise** — +0.0163 turns at d3 is +13 turns over
800 games against a quantum of 0.00125. That is the expected shape: the walk's visits now come out of
the same allowance the nodes do, so an honestly-charged b10 decision explores less than a free-walk
b10 one. The Melira precedent is the same sign (613 → 370 s for +0.28 t) and the user reverted a
default-flip of it on 2026-09-22.

INCLUDES a walk visit, so charged and uncharged units are different currencies (charged b40 reads
3.16x base units for 0.60x the CPU). `ms` is the only cross-arm cost figure here, which is why the
arms are interleaved in one pool.

### What a default would be, and what still gates it

The shape of the change is the user's own 2026-09 direction: **charge for the work, then recalibrate
`SearchBudget::NODES_PER_VIRTUAL_MS` (900) so the median decision keeps its allowance** — i.e. 900 →
~3,600 together with `MTG_SOLVE_CHARGE` default ON, so every deck's existing `--budget-ms` keeps
buying the same REAL search while the budget starts binding on the part that actually costs.

Note how close the walk charge already is to the user's words ("charge a weighted amount for payment
solves alongside the node"): §4 shows payments are ~1 per scored subset, so charging the walk IS
charging the payments, one filter stage earlier. A per-PAYMENT charge (or a weighted one) is the
natural refinement — it would track cost even better, since the current lever bills all 17.9 M
*visits* rather than the 3.3 M that survive to a payment.

## 6. What is left, ranked

1. **Pin each deck's parity budget under the charge, on held-out seeds (§5).** This is the whole
   recovery: Snow at 0.60–0.83x repays the 27% with interest (1.274 × 0.60 = 0.76) and several other
   decks come along for free. It is a fleet-wide GT rebaseline and therefore the user's call, and it
   should land AFTER the uncommitted `ACT_LINE_HOLD` rebaseline so the two GT moves stay separable.
2. **Payment call volume (§4).** ~45% of CPU, 24–37 solves per charged unit, 2.27 M scored subsets
   per decision. Nothing here is a hot function to optimise; the question is how many of those 2.27 M
   subsets need a payment solve at all, and whether a result can be reused across them. Note the mana
   cache already exists (`ManaCacheKey` folds the reserve set as a per-source *eligibility* term) and
   `MTG_TAP_STATS` reports the greedy solver carrying 84.9% with the backtracker at 15.1%.
3. **The discarded 10.1% (§2b).** A decision that spends 25x its budget and is rolled back whole is
   the clearest waste in the census. The fix is not a smaller ceiling (that loses the pass outright);
   it is a pass-start estimate in the right currency — which is what §5 makes possible, because the
   estimate is denominated in the units the charge corrects.
4. **`MTG_SNOW_LOOK_COLOR`** — 26% of site-8 opens on the heavy decision fail the colour test
   (32,411 of 123,968), against the 13.2% fleet average that was measured before. Already measured
   adoptable on 1,440 held-out games (0 cells worse, −0.58% CPU); still not flipped only because GT
   carries the uncommitted `ACT_LINE_HOLD` rebaseline and the two would entangle.
5. **The silent activation drops** — 44,845 in one decision, 13.4% of enumerated activations. Both
   remaining defects are diagnosed and unbuilt in `trailing-activation-payment-hole.md` (the
   continuation double-book; generic/`{S}` never held).

## 7. THE FIX — the plan, in order (USER: *"Let's compact and then fix this"*, 2026-10-01)

The measurements above are done; this section is the build. The goal is the user's own 2026-09
direction, now with numbers behind it: **charge what the work costs, then set the allowance so the
median decision keeps what it had** — and the fleet table in §5 says the recalibration must not be a
single uniform multiple, because 4x makes every deck but Snow 1.21–1.55x dearer.

**Step 1 — give the charge a WEIGHT (small code change).** Today `MTG_SOLVE_CHARGE` bills 1 unit per
`consider` visit, which is a 1:1 exchange rate asserted in a comment ("one subset visit costs ~1-2 us,
one budget unit is calibrated at ~1.1 us"). Make it a ratio: `MTG_SOLVE_CHARGE_W` = visits per charged
unit (W=1 reproduces today exactly, so the default is byte-identical). This is the dial that decouples
Snow from the fleet: Snow's cost is walk-dominated (§4), so a heavier walk weight bites Snow hardest
while a node-dominated deck barely notices. Charging at the PAYMENT site instead is the other form of
the same idea and is closer to the user's words — §4 shows payments are ~1 per scored subset, i.e. one
filter stage past the visit, so the two differ by the 6:1 ratio between visits (17.9 M) and scored
subsets (3.3 M). Price both; prefer whichever needs less recalibration elsewhere.

**Step 2 — sweep (weight × allowance) on the fleet, held-out seeds, ONE pooled batch.** Arms carried
as per-job `heurarm` flags + per-job `budget_ms`, never one process per arm. Decks: the §5 six plus
the cheap remainder of the suite. Accept the point where **no deck is worse** and total CPU is lowest;
report Snow's own ratio there separately, since it is the deck the campaign is for.
Required by `replicate-trades-before-ruling`: the winner is re-measured on fresh seeds before it is
proposed — 3 of 6 adoptable-looking trades have died at that step before.

**Step 3 — flip defaults and rebaseline, in this order.**
1. **Commit the uncommitted `ACT_LINE_HOLD` GT rebaseline FIRST.** It is already in the tree (610 keys,
   3 one-line notes, `check_gt_logs` 610/0/0). Landing a second GT move on top makes the two
   inseparable, and that is the one thing that cannot be undone by re-running.
2. Flip `MTG_SOLVE_CHARGE` (+ its weight) and write each deck's recalibrated allowance where the
   ENGINE reads it — `value_play` in `decks/<D>/<D>.value.json` is the shipped play budget; the suite
   cells in `test/regression_cases.sh` are the TEST's pinned budgets and move only if the tier's
   coverage intent changes. Do not conflate the two.
3. Rebaseline all three tiers, `python3 test/check_gt_logs.py`, then push and report CI per-OS
   (determinism parity is the job that matters).

**Step 4 — re-read the diagnostics that the fix invalidates.** The charge changes what a unit MEANS,
so every unit-denominated number in this repo's docs becomes a pre-fix number. In particular the
census's `xbud` column (§2a) and the 25x overrun guard are both denominated in units, so the
pass-start estimate they feed is exactly what the charge corrects — re-read `idwaste_units` (§2b, the
discarded 10.1%) AFTER the flip rather than treating it as a separate item to fix.

**What is explicitly NOT part of this fix:** bottoming (the incoming keep table removes it);
`bottom_eval_units: 900` (same reason — it would move GT to buy something about to be free);
`MTG_FOLD_ODO_SKIP` (already built, default ON, byte-identical, worth 1.3%).

## 8. THE FIX, as built — and a measurement bug that re-prices §5

Written after building step 1 and running step 2. Read §8.2 before trusting any core-ms ratio
anywhere above: one of the apparatus bugs it describes is large enough to have produced §5's

### 8.1 Built: the charge is now a RATE, not an assertion

`MTG_SOLVE_CHARGE_W` = **visits per charged unit** (default 1, which is byte-identical to the old
charge: verified digest AND units equal, `logs/snowperf/wcheck.sh`). Per-job as
`"solve_charge_w": N` beside `"flags": {"MTG_SOLVE_CHARGE": true}` — a numeric lever, so it lives in
`valuearm::Arm` rather than `heurarm::Arm`, which is boolean-only. Three details that are load-bearing:

* **Why the weight and not the budget.** A charged decision spends `nodes + visits/W` against a limit
  `B`. Raising `B` relaxes *both* terms, which is exactly why §5's uniform-4x recalibration made every
  deck but Snow 1.21–1.55x dearer — it bought node-dominated decks more search. Raising `W` relaxes
  only the walk. So the sweep holds every deck at its own cell and moves `W`.
* **The residual is scoped, not left running.** A `thread_local` visit counter that outlived its host
  frame would carry visits between GAMES on a reused batch worker, making the charge depend on which
  games shared that thread — the nondeterminism `SearchBudget` exists to remove. The budget-path
  residual is saved/zeroed/restored by `GreedyChargeGuard`; the decision-meter path's lives in
  `decisionwork` and is reset by `Begin()`/`Scope`.
* **The exhaustion test moved INTO the charge.** `Consume` + `Exhausted` + `Overrun` is ~15
  instructions over four thread_locals, and it ran once per subset visit — 20 M of them in one Snow
  decision. Measured: billed per visit it cost KittyEquipment **1.08–1.14x core-ms for a digest that
  never moved**, and pirates 1.32x — decks the lever cannot help, paying the accounting in full.
  Polling only on a charged visit (so the walk notices exhaustion up to W−1 visits late, bounded and
  deterministic) removed every one of those regressions: kitty 1.143x → 1.002x at W=64.

### 8.2 THE APPARATUS BUG: a pooled A/B must emit its arms INNERMOST

`holdab.sh`, `chargeab.sh`, `recal.sh` and `fleetcharge.sh` all emit every chunk of one arm before
any chunk of the next. The batch scheduler's cost proxy is identical across arms of a cell, so ties
dispatch in **manifest order** — the first-emitted arm runs into the pool's crunch and the
last-emitted one as the pool drains. `holdab.sh`'s own header claims "the arms share the pool, the
box and the moment"; the ordering is what makes that false.

Measured, on a 1,296-job sweep run both ways (same seeds, same binary):

| ordering | provably-inert null arm, core-ms vs baseline |
|---|---|
| arms OUTERMOST | median **0.799x**, min 0.132x, over 160 arms |
| arms INNERMOST | median **0.997x**, pooled **0.9974x**, over 54 cells |

So the bias was of the same order as the effects being measured, and it flatters whichever arm is
emitted last. **Every core-ms ratio in §5 was measured arms-outermost with the charged arm last.**

Two rules this earns, both of which cost a re-run to learn:

* **Emit the arms of one (deck, cell, seed) consecutively.** Then they land on different workers in
  the same instant, which is the property one pooled batch is *for*.
* **Carry a NULL ARM, and do not substitute digest equality for it.** The obvious control — "the
  digests match, so the work matched, so the ms ratio is noise" — is *wrong here*, because deleting
  work without changing play is precisely what the lever claims to do. The valid control is an arm
  that is inert by construction (charge off + a weight the charge cannot read, verified equal in
  digest and units) measured in the same scheduling position. Its distance from 1.000 is the bar.

**What it does NOT change: the answer to the user's question.** Re-measured with the arms paired,
same seeds, same cells (`logs/snowperf/holdab2.sh`, 1,920 paired games): `MTG_ACT_LINE_HOLD` costs
**1.288x pooled** (d2/b1 1.247x, d3/b10 1.307x, d5/b20 1.302x, quality −0.0025/−0.0038/+0.0031).
The honest figure is marginally *worse* than the 1.274x reported in §1, not better.

0.915 units on the population replicates the A/B's 0.916 exactly. Two readings beyond that:

**It is a tail effect, not a broad one.** Per-seed, the median units ratio is **0.9999**: 589 of the
1,280 games are byte-identical in units, 659 fall, and 32 RISE (worst 2.573x). That is the correct
shape for this lever -- it removes redundant plans, and half of Snow's games never build a redundant
plan set big enough to matter. It also means a small sample of Snow games can read this lever as
zero, which is what makes the heavy-tail pairing in §11.5 the honest measurement rather than a
supplementary one.

**The freed budget is respent on the WALK.** `sub_entered` and `u_greedy_walk` both go UP ~4% while
plans emitted fall 24%: plans per odometer position drops 0.148 -> 0.108. Nothing regressed -- this is
`matrix-cost-is-abandonment-rate` again, a budget is a work CAP and freed work is re-spent. It is
also why the budget-discipline ladder barely moves (decisions over 1x budget 25.57% -> 24.62%): the
fold makes each decision cheaper per plan, not cheaper than its allowance.

Re-work fell by more than the work did: overrun-DISCARDED units 3,159,449 -> **1,127,015** (0.357x,
2.35% -> 0.92% of all units), and aborted passes 14 -> 5 of which the discard threw away a PROVEN
better win 11 -> **4**. That last number is a correctness-adjacent improvement, not a cost one.

### 12.2 THE LARGEST UNEXAMINED COST: a board copy that answers "no" 80% of the time

`SubsetPayableWithFilters` is the real-payment RESCUE: when the flat mana model rejects a subset and
the board holds any filter source, the walk runs an actual payment to see whether a re-colouring
filter saves it. **It copies the board per call.** The engine's own comment
(`enumstats::g_c_resc_call`) predicted the failure mode exactly -- *"`armed` is a board/hand fact, so
it can be true for a whole enumeration while no individual subset can use it, and then every
flat-mana failure buys a board copy that was always going to say no"* -- and asked for calls vs
rescues to tell those apart. On the population:

| | baseline | adopted |
|---|---|---|
| rescue calls | 84,749,576 | **88,334,626** |
| rescued | 16,517,323 | 17,646,498 |
| **rescue rate** | 19.5% | **20.0%** |
| **wasted board copies** | 68,232,253 | **70,688,128** |
| calls per unit | 0.631 | **0.719** |

**0.72 board copies per charged unit, 80% of which answer no**, and the fold made it slightly WORSE
in absolute terms because the walk now visits more subsets. On the heaviest single decision (seed
910312 t6) it is 53,665 calls for 13,981 rescues. This is per-unit cost, so unlike a plan-count
saving the budget cannot see it at all -- it makes every unit dearer rather than spending more units.

A shortcut already exists and is **default OFF**: `MTG_RESCUE_TOTAL_GATE` skips the rescue when the
flat failure is a TOTAL shortfall (the board provably cannot fund the subset at all, which is not a
colour question and so beyond a re-colouring filter's help). Its soundness has a self-check built in
(`g_c_resc_tot_ok` must stay 0) and the first formulation FAILED it -- 346,662,289 total-short calls
of which 54,226,344 were rescued anyway, because `Total()` on the debited and noncreature-only pools
is not an upper bound on what the real payment can tap. Only the raw undebited pool clause survived.
**This is the next lever to measure on Snow, and it is sized here for the first time.**

### 12.3 The chain arm: collapsing duplicates was the WRONG lever, and that is now settled

§11.8 ranked the chain slot's `covered` cell (5,076 candidates at 98.5% duplicate) as residual #3,
"lossless, still not built". It is built (`MTG_BP_W0_CHAIN_COLLAPSE`, identity proven for `0 <= ci <
W`, deliberately not for `ci < 0`) -- and `per-decision-work-census.md` had already measured that
collapsing it is worth **-0.03% units** while simply not emitting the arm (`MTG_BP_CHAIN_SLOT=0`) is
worth **-1.88% units and -10.72% `cand_scored`** with win turns identical chunk by chunk over 150
games. `past_w`, the case the arm exists for, is **0.20%** on Snow.

So residual #3 as stated was the wrong target by 60x, for a reason worth stating generally: **a
duplicate STATE is not a removable CANDIDATE.** The apply that discovers the duplicate has already
been paid for by the time the census can see it; the only saving is in never generating the
candidate. The chain slot stays ON fleet-wide -- it is load-bearing on Dragonstorm, where
claude_s1_gi0 and claude_s26_gi25 both go T4 -> T5 without it (bisected to a rank-32-of-47
continuation) -- so this is a per-deck question, now expressed as
`DecisionProvider::BpChainSlotOptIn` with the per-job numeric arm `bp_chain_slot` for the A/B.
Held-out replication: `logs/snowperf/chainab.py`.

### 12.4 RETRACTION: the three "clear win" charge adoptions of §10.3

§10.3 lists giants (0.882x), mirrorwing (0.854x) and creature_giving (0.945x) as CLEAR WINs,

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| creature_giving | w4 g3 | 0.954 | 1.010 | 1.002 | -1 game |

creature_giving's g5 saving (0.938) IS its null arm (0.935). §10.3's figures pooled the two cells,
which hid that one cell of each deck is entirely apparatus. Two separate errors:

1. **Pooling cells hides a per-cell null.** The null arm is a PER-CELL error bar (it measures that
   cell's cold start and pool position); a pooled effect compared against nothing is not a verdict.
2. **`units` IS NOT COMPARABLE ACROSS CHARGE ON/OFF**, and I used it as a gate without noticing.

bucket, subtract `u_greedy_walk` from the charged arm's total and compare work at all the OTHER
sites. That quantity is deterministic, immune to scheduling, and means the same thing in both arms.
Any future charge adoption on these decks must clear that gate, not the ms column.

Nothing was shipped on the retracted numbers -- the three provider edits were still outstanding when
the re-read happened, which is the only reason this is a retraction and not a regression.

### 12.7 WHAT REMAINS ON THE DUPLICATE-PLAN SIDE (complete, as of 2026-10-01)

Six items, ordered by what I would do next. The first is correctness, not performance.

1. **A KNOWN-FALSE IDENTITY IS SHIPPING DEFAULT-ON.** `MTG_BP_W0_NOBP` (default ON since
   2026-09-23) skips a breakpoint variant whose base plan's apply reached no breakpoint. At
   breakpoint **site 9** a base plan is structurally BLIND -- its zero means "cannot see this", not
   "nothing to decide" -- so the skip deletes real lines: **25 mismatches of 41**, with two fixtures
   losing a turn at an UNBOUNDED budget (where there is no freed work to re-spend, so it cannot be
   explained away as churn). The repair exists and is **default OFF** (`MTG_BP_NOBP_SITE9`), because
   tightening a default-ON skip moves the committed line on every budgeted cell of every deck. The
   inertness argument is that all 118 fixtures are byte-identical with the arm disabled -- which
   establishes latency **on the fixture set**, not on the budgeted suite cells, and the fixtures
   escape only because their committed decision is taken in FSLineWin, the host the shipped skip
   never reached. **This is the one genuinely unfinished item rather than an untaken win.**
2. **The fleet chain screen above** -- seven decks at >=1,000 chain applies per 12 games with
   `past_W` of 0.00-1.34%, each needing its own A/B (kitty has the most volume and reads exactly
   0.00%). `chainab.py` generalises to any deck by changing one dict key.
3. **`MTG_BP_W0_CHAIN_COLLAPSE` is unadopted and now ORPHANED on Snow.** It is sound, verified, and
   worth -0.03% units -- on Snow it is moot (the arm is no longer emitted), and on the decks that
   KEEP the arm nobody has measured it. It is the only collapse here with a proof behind it.
4. **`MTG_BP_W0_FSW=3` is an unadopted QUALITY win, not a cost lever.** -0.26% units / +0.15%
   candidates is a wash, but smoke reads **slower=0, faster=5, play-changed=6**. It is at 0 and the
   case for it is on a different axis from everything else in this document, which is probably why
   it has been passed over twice.
5. **36.8% of candidates still collapse** (12.5). The fold took the copy-axis share to zero; what
   remains is a different, unnamed axis. Naming it is the next diagnosis, and `MTG_CAND_CENSUS`'s
   bucketed-distinct columns are the instrument.
6. **Merge debt:** `shapestats::Slot::rej_dup_s1` is now redundant with the upstream `e_rej_dup`
   per-walk funnel (both count site 1's dup rejections). The census field table should move onto the
   e_* funnel and drop the flat twin; kept only so `sub_rej_dupsrc_s1` does not silently read zero.

Closed, with proofs, and NOT to be re-proposed: the `rank` and `unif` arms as cost levers
(`bp_choice` is a fixed-width positional index, so an upstream prune only swaps occupants); the
rank-0 identity as stated (58.7%, refuted -- the missing condition was `g_rollout_nest`); and

### 12.9 THE SITE-9 REPAIR WAS NEVER WIRED INTO THE SHIPPED ARM

12.7's item 1 said `MTG_BP_NOBP_SITE9` was a default-OFF repair for a known-false identity. Reading
the code to measure it showed something worse: **the flag did nothing to the shipped arm at all.**

`NobpSite9FixOn()` had exactly one reference -- inside `NobpSite9Watch()`, which enables the
battlefield scan that SETS `g_bp_searchonly_suppressed`. The only consumer of that flag was
`w0collapse::Memo::Record`, i.e. the FSLineWin path. The shipped arm's own recording in
`SolveWithLookahead`:

    if ((nobp_here || w0nobp_here) && pl.bp_choice < 0 && g_bp_any_last == any_before)
    { bp_nobp.insert(cand_index); }

never consulted it. So the flag's own comment -- *"close the hole in the SHIPPED arm too"* -- was
false in the tree: it armed a watch whose answer nothing in that host read.

**Now wired**, with the per-apply reset it needs (the flag is `thread_local` and set-only at the
gate, so without a reset the first suppressing apply would make every later base plan on that thread
look suppressed -- which would silently DISARM the whole NOBP skip instead of tightening it). Still
default OFF: this host's skip is default ON, so tightening it skips strictly less and moves the
committed line on every budgeted cell of every deck.

**And verified to actually fire**, because fixing a no-op into another no-op is the obvious failure
mode here (`digest-equality-can-mean-broken`). Plain vs `MTG_BP_NOBP_SITE9=1`, 25 games at d3/b10:

| deck | repair live? | avg turns |
|---|---|---|
| critter | **yes** (solve-memo 49,741 -> 48,942 misses) | 4.5200 both |
| dragonstorm | **yes** (28,683 -> 28,728) | 4.5600 both |
| snow | **yes** (160,856 -> 161,273) | 5.8000 both |
| melira | **yes** (51,448 -> 51,248) | 4.5200 both |
| auras | no -- gate never fires here | 4.0400 both |

The work moves on four of five decks while the average winning turn holds, which is what a

### 12.10 The site-9 repair, PRICED -- and it is a thin trade, so it is the user's call

With the repair wired (12.9) and given a heurarm slot so both arms share one pool, it can finally be
measured. First the cheap question -- where does the gate fire at all? (`site9screen.py`, 25 games,
whole-output identity rather than the average, since a tightening can move the committed line while
leaving the average flat):

**The gate fires on 11 of 27 decks** (angels, critter, dragons, dragonstorm, fivecolour, fungus,
goblins, melira, minotaur, selesnya, snow) and the other **16 are byte-identical** and need no A/B.
The average winning turn was unchanged on all 11 at that sample.

Then the price, over those 11 decks only: two cells, 800 games per arm per cell, arms innermost,
null arm per deck, held-out seeds 2105000+ -- **17,600 games**:

| | result |
|---|---|
| units | **+0.2% to +3.8%** (melira g3 1.0380, minotaur g3 1.0229, angels g3 1.0192; most +0.3-1%) |
| quality, pooled | **-3.0 games of 17,600** (negative = turns won back) |
| per deck | angels **-2.0**, fivecolour -1.0, melira -1.0, dragonstorm **+1.0**, eight decks exactly 0 |

**This is a TRADE and therefore NOT adopted** -- `units` regresses, so it is not the no-drawback
case that `adopt-clean-wins-without-asking` pre-approves. Staged with its price attached:

* **The case FOR adopting:** the identity being removed is KNOWN FALSE. The engine currently ships a
  default-ON skip that deletes reachable lines, and the sharpest evidence is two fixtures losing a
  turn at an **UNBOUNDED** budget -- where nothing can be recovered by re-spending freed work. The
  measured quality sign here is favourable (-3.0 net, the only positive being a single quantum on
  dragonstorm), and ~0.8% of units is a small price for removing a false identity.
* **The case AGAINST:** -3.0 games of 17,600 is 0.017%, which is at the metric's own quantum, and
  adopting moves GT on 11 decks for an effect barely distinguishable from noise at the suite's
  BUDGETED cells.
* **Why the budgeted cells understate it, which is the thing to weigh:** a budget is a work CAP, so
  a line this repair preserves competes for the same allowance and the gain is partly absorbed
  (`matrix-cost-is-abandonment-rate`). The fixtures that exposed the defect failed at an unbounded
  budget precisely because that is where a deleted line cannot be recovered. So the honest reading is
  that this measurement is a LOWER bound on the correctness benefit and a fair estimate of the cost.

My recommendation is to take it -- an engine should not ship a skip whose identity is known false,
and 0.8% units buys that -- but it regresses an axis, so it is staged rather than adopted.
